What is my collection worth, and how could I sell it?

What it's worth. Every price in Grimoire is the card's market price: roughly what the card sells for between people online. To look up one card without adding it, type its name in the Check a price box at the top of the Dashboard. Your collection's total is on the Dashboard and in My cards (Inventory in full mode).

Condition. Grimoire shows Near Mint prices, and the condition you pick for a card doesn't change the value shown. A worn card usually sells for less than the number you see, so treat the total as a ceiling, not a promise.

Market price is not what a store pays. A local store has to resell the card at a profit, so its offer is usually well under market price (store credit is often a better deal than cash). Selling to a person yourself gets you closer to market price, but costs you time, fees and postage.

Ways to sell

  • A local game store. Quick and simple: bring the cards, get cash or credit the same day. You get the lowest price.
  • A marketplace such as Manapool. Buyers find your cards online and you ship them. You get closer to market price and pay a fee. See Selling on Manapool.
  • Friends and trading. Swap cards with someone you know, no money involved (Friends & trading).

Unsure what to sell? Sort My cards by value and start at the top: a few valuable cards are often worth more than a whole bulk box.

2. Locations & putaway rules

Location settings

A location is anything that holds cards.

  • Card ceiling: the most cards it holds. When full, putaway skips it.
  • Max per card: the most copies of one card it takes. A one-of-each binder uses 1.
  • Priority: breaks ties when rules match equally. Higher wins.
  • Pick priority: which location's copies are counted or pulled first. Higher wins.
  • Sorting tray: cards wait here until you empty it. It's never a putaway destination.
  • Sorting method: the order cards sit in. Pick a ready-made one such as Set code A–Z, then collector #, or choose Custom… and add levels (set, collector number, name, mana value, color, rarity and more). Pick lists, Inventory (filtered to that location) and Putaway then follow this order.
  • Cards per page: binders only. Pockets per side of a sheet (9 unless you change it). Putaway uses it to name the page.
  • Sell from this location: off by default. Turn it on for places you ship from. Listings only list copies here, and the Manapool order matcher only pulls stock from here. Copies elsewhere still count toward a rule's Keep. Leave it off for personal binders, decks and sorting trays.

Putaway rules

Open a location's Edit & rules to say what belongs there: color identity, rarity, type, set, price range and more.

  • A group is ALL (and) or ANY (or) of its conditions. NOT flips it.
  • + Add group nests another group. Example: (Set is neo OR Set is mh3) AND Rarity is common.
  • A location with putaway on and no rules takes everything. It's your overflow box and always loses to a real rule.
  • When several locations match equally, Priority decides.

3. The Putaway page

Putaway uses your rules to answer "where does this go?":

  • Empty a bin: the usual step after receiving into a sorting tray. Pick the tray (any location with cards works), review the plan, tick what to move, and file it.
  • Fill a bin: shows which cards elsewhere a bin's rules would claim.
  • Consolidate: gathers a card split across many locations into one. It defaults to where most copies already sit. Change a row's Target to send them elsewhere.
  • Check one card: shows where a card would go and why. Nothing moves. File now adds a card that isn't in inventory yet.
  • Whole collection: re-checks every stack against current rules (use it after changing a rule) and moves the ones that no longer fit. Stacks move whole.
  • Rule breakers: finds cards that don't match the rules of the bin they sit in, and moves them out.
  • Capacity: how full each location is.

Where inside a sorted location

If the destination has a sorting method, the tabs above also say where in it the card goes: the card it follows (or "at the start" or "at the end") and a rough position, like ELD section: cards 234–284, this one about #251. In a binder it reads as pages instead, like pages 26–32, this one about page 28, pocket 8.

The Show where it goes button draws the box or binder with the spot marked. It's a guess to start from. A card filed out of order throws it off.

4. Receiving cards

In Simple mode this page is called Add cards and shows only the add-a-card part.

Finding this on a real card

Searching by name is enough most of the time. Check the card only when several printings come up, or when you enter a set code and collector number:

Foil is shiny and reflective. Nonfoil is flat and matte. Etched foil looks textured.

Where it came from

The row at the top of the page picks the source. Each one shows only what it needs.

  • Already mine: cards you already own. They go in with no cost, which is honest: you aren't pretending they were free. Search by name, type the set and collector number, or Paste a list (which also takes a card list file). The Added list is the record. New stacks use the condition in Settings → Preferences.
  • Bought: a purchase, such as a box from another collector. Everything you add lands on a lot receipt. Enter the total you spent (shipping included) and it's spread across the lines by market value (or evenly), so every card gets a cost. Type a Cost each on a line to set that one yourself; it comes off the top and the rest is spread. You can keep several receipts open at once, one tab each. Drafts autosave and follow you between devices. Nothing reaches inventory until you press Post receipt.
  • Mana Pool order: cards and sealed product you bought on Mana Pool. Connect your Mana Pool account first in Settings → Integrations. Your purchases are listed newest first (Load older orders for more). An order bought from several sellers comes in several packages: press Receive on one package as it arrives, or Receive all. Grimoire makes a lot receipt with every card's printing, finish, condition, language and quantity filled in. Each line costs what you paid for it plus a share of the shipping and tax, so the receipt adds up to what you paid. Anything refunded is left off. Pick where it goes first (Put everything in, or the putaway rules), and change any line before you post, as with any lot.
    • Already in your inventory? A card you already have in the same printing, finish, condition and language gets a note and a choice: Add as new copies, or Already have it, just set the cost (when you added these very cards earlier with no cost: it puts the cost on them and adds nothing). If some of those copies were added with no cost on or after the order date, Grimoire picks Already have it for you; otherwise (including copies from another package of the same order) it picks Add as new copies.
    • Not in Grimoire's catalog? A line Grimoire can't match is shown in red and stops the receipt posting. Note its cost, remove it, and add it by hand under Bought with that amount in Cost each.
    • Received once. A package already posted shows Received as and its receipt number, and can't be received again. The receipt's Source is the Mana Pool order number.
    • Also mark received on Mana Pool (ticked at first): when you post, Grimoire tells Mana Pool the packages arrived. If Mana Pool says no, your receipt is still saved; mark it received on Mana Pool's site instead.
    • If Mana Pool turns down Grimoire's connection, reconnect it in Settings → Integrations (how).
  • File: a transfer file from another Grimoire user, or a TCGplayer Pull Sheet CSV. A card list from ManaBox, Moxfield or a spreadsheet goes in under Already mine → Paste a list instead.

Keeping a choice between cards

Adding a run of the same kind of card? Tick the lock beside Finish, Condition, Language or Location and it keeps that choice after each add. Anything not locked goes back to its usual start. Your locks and what they hold are remembered on this device, and each person has their own. Tab skips the locks, including Keep set code after adding, so it still goes straight from box to box; click a lock to change it.

  • Finish: if a printing doesn't come in your locked finish, that card goes in as the finish it has, and a note under the box or the status line says so. Your locked finish comes back for the next card.
  • Language: in Search by name, a printing that doesn't exist in your locked language stops with the usual message, so pick a different language or printing. In Set + number, the card goes in as that language with the default print's details, and the status line says so.
  • Location: a locked location beats Location from the putaway rules.

Cards or sealed product

The Card / Sealed switch on the add box picks what you're adding. For sealed product (boxes, bundles and the like) search the product by name, then pick a quantity and location. Under Already mine it goes straight into Sealed with no cost. Under Bought it joins the lot receipt next to any cards, and the money you spent is spread across all of them. With the switch on Sealed, the Added list shows sealed product. Simple mode hides the switch.

File and list formats

  • Your own CSV: one row per stack. Columns match by name, in any order. Each row needs a Card Name, or a Set and Collector #. Optional: Storage Location or Binder Name (if missing or blank you pick one destination), Foil or Finish (normal, foil, etched), Condition (defaults to your preference), Language (en), Purchase Price (becomes the cost), Price, Quantity (1) and Scryfall ID. A row with a name but no collector number is matched by name, using the Set (code or full name) if there is one. Where several printings fit, or there is no set, the preview shows a dropdown for you to choose, and a name that matches no card is skipped. If a row names a card but its set and number belong to a different card, the row is skipped too, and the preview says which card that number really is. Skipped rows can be downloaded or copied to fix and import again. Importing a file that matches one from the last 30 days asks first.
  • A ManaBox export: in ManaBox, export your collection as CSV and choose it here. Binder names become locations, Purchase price becomes the cost, and the Foil column (normal, foil, etched) is read.
  • A Moxfield or Archidekt CSV: export the collection or deck as CSV. Counts, names, editions, conditions and purchase prices come across.
  • A plain spreadsheet: save it as CSV with columns like Card, Set, Qty and Foil?. Full set names such as "Commander Masters" work, and no collector number is needed.
  • A pasted list: one card per line from a deck site such as Moxfield or Archidekt, like 4 Lightning Bolt (M10) 146. Each line needs the set and collector number. Lines without them are listed so you can fix and paste again.
  • A transfer file: from Inventory → Export → Transfer file. It holds printings, quantities and, if the sender chose, their cost.
  • A TCGplayer Pull Sheet: the CSV from Manage Lists → Pull Sheet. It has no locations, so every card goes to the one you pick.

Counting a box into a sorting tray

Choose a sorting tray as the location. Cards land there unfiled. When you finish, empty the tray and they go where your rules say.

Receiving on the floor

To have a purchase booked in on a phone, use Received on the floor under Bought: fill in where it came from, what you spent and how to spread it, and press Open for Warehouse Mode. It gets its receipt number now, and anyone can book cards and sealed onto it in Warehouse Mode, several people at once. Nobody there sees what you spent.

The table shows how many are booked, how many are still in a tray waiting to be put away, and who booked them. Booked copies are in stock right away, but they aren't picked for orders or listed for sale until you press Close. Until then Card Inventory marks them Held on LOT-… until the desk closes it, and an order line that's short because of them says so too. Closing spreads the money across everything booked, the same way a lot receipt does, even if you corrected a booked card's condition or finish in the meantime, and the receipt joins Receipt history. Copies still in a tray stay there as ordinary stock, and the close says how many. A receipt with nothing booked can be removed instead. Mana Pool orders stay here at the desk.

5. Reading the inventory grid

In Simple mode this page is called My cards and lists your cards one row per printing and location.

Inventory has three views of the same cards. Switch with the toggle at the top.

  • By stack: one row per printing, condition and location. Use it to change quantity, move a stack or edit condition. Allocated is how many are on a pick list and not yet shipped.
  • By card name: one row per name across everything. Click a row to see its stacks.
  • Storefront: one row per printing, language, finish and condition, with location ignored. This is how Manapool sells. It's the only place to change the listed quantity with −/+.

On a By stack row, the small map pin shows where the card sits in its box or binder when that location has a sorting method. If it doesn't, the pin is dimmed (full mode): press it to open that location's setup at Sorting method. In simple mode the spot stays empty, so the buttons line up on every row.

Listed is stock offered for sale on Manapool. A card can be listed with none allocated, or allocated with none listed.

Sold 7d (By stack and Storefront) is how many copies of that printing sold on Manapool in the last 7 days, counted the same way as on Movers: every finish, condition and language of the printing together, ≥ for "at least", and a short FAST mark where Movers says SELLING FAST: it's selling well above its usual pace. A dash means there's no sales history yet or the printing couldn't be matched to Manapool. To see only those cards, tick Selling fast under Flags in Query.

Lending cards

Lent a card to a friend? Press Manage on it, then Lend… and enter how many, who to (type any name; they don't need an account) and, if you like, a give back by date. No emails are sent, and nothing shows on their side.

Lent copies stay in your total and your value, and the card shows a Lent to Sam tag. Until they're back they aren't picked, listed for sale, offered in trades, pulled into a deck or counted in a cycle count.

Lent out at the top of this page lists everything you have out, overdue first. A loan past its date says overdue, and the Dashboard reminds you. When a card comes back, press Mark returned there or in Manage. Got only some back? Change the number beside the button.

Query (top of Inventory, and Statistics) filters on almost anything (name, set, type, rules text, colors, price, quantity, release date) and shows a live count. Two fields use Scryfall's Tagger: Oracle tag (what a card does, like removal) and Art tag (what the art shows, like dragon). Type the exact tag name from tagger.scryfall.com. If it isn't a real tag, you're told.

6. Selling on Manapool

Manapool is the online marketplace your buyers use. Grimoire sends it your stock and prices.

Connect your account

Grimoire connects through Mana Pool's own sign-in, so you never paste a password or access token into Grimoire.

  1. Go to Settings → Integrations and press Connect Mana Pool.
  2. Mana Pool's own page opens. Sign in there if it asks, check the list of what Grimoire may do (your listings, your orders and shipping, your purchases), and press its allow button.
  3. You come back to Settings, which says Connected.
  • Disconnect (same place) ends the connection on both sides. You can also end it on Mana Pool, under Integrations → Connected apps. Either way, Grimoire stops reading your orders and changing your listings until you connect again; your listings on Mana Pool stay as they are.
  • Reconnect Mana Pool: if Mana Pool ends the connection (you removed it there, or it went unused for about a month), every page shows a reminder and the button says Reconnect Mana Pool. Press it and allow again.
  • Another app manages your Mana Pool listings: Mana Pool lets only one tool change a seller's listings. If another tool already does, Grimoire asks again without that permission (or, if Mana Pool just says no, Settings offers Connect without listing changes) and connects for orders, shipping and purchases only. Push and Delist on Listings and Bulk Price are then greyed out with that reason.
  • Still on an old access token? Accounts that pasted an email and access token before get a reminder on every page with the date the old token stops working, seven days after the switch. Connect before then. Connecting deletes the old token at once; after the date it's deleted anyway, and Mana Pool features stop until you connect.
  • If Connect Mana Pool is greyed out, an administrator must first switch Mana Pool on and set up Grimoire's Mana Pool app (Admin → Mana Pool).

Three pages, three jobs

  • Inventory → Storefront (§5): change one listing's quantity by hand. + lists a copy from a sell-from location. If every copy is in another location, + sends nothing and tells you how many are still listed there.
  • Listings sets quantity by rule.
    1. Make rules with the same builder as putaway. Each has a Keep number: copies you hold back. The rest is the listed target.
    2. Set a default keep for anything no rule names.
    3. Press Evaluate.
    4. Tick rows, then Apply ticked (changes Grimoire only) or Push ticked to Manapool (updates the live listing; listings Manapool already shows at that quantity and price are skipped).
    Only sell-from locations are listed. Each Evaluate row is one Manapool listing, added up over all locations. Price is what a push will send. Sold 7d is copies of that printing sold on Manapool in the last 7 days (as on Movers), and Selling fast only keeps just the listings marked FAST (selling fast). It's greyed out until there's two weeks of sales history to compare. Days left is about how many days the copies listed on Manapool right now would last at the last 7 days' pace (see Movers); click its header to put the shortest first. Delist all from Manapool sets every live listing to zero now. Delist unprofitable (Manapool tab) takes down only the card listings priced under your minimum profit; cards with no cost entered stay up. The Never list below my minimum profit switch (Rules tab) makes Evaluate give those same cards a target of 0, shown as Below minimum profit in the Rule column. Minimum profit (% of cost) (Rules tab, next to the switch) sets what counts as unprofitable for both: the price minus your average cost, as a % of cost, before Manapool's fees. At 25%, a $1.00 card must list at $1.25 or more. It starts at 25%, or 0% if you had the switch on before this setting came in.
  • The Card pricing tab on Listings (the old Bulk Price page) sets price by rule. A rule's mode is percent of a base price, delta (base plus or minus an amount), fixed (a flat $) or exclude (carves an exception out of a lower-priority rule; those stacks go out at market price × the condition %). Only stacks with a listed quantity are priced. The base is Scryfall's market price, Manapool's live price, today's TCGplayer, Card Kingdom or Manapool price, or the highest or lowest of those three. The asking price is never saved. Grimoire works it out from your rules and the latest price each time. If copies match different rules, the highest-priority rule prices the listing. Evaluate shows each Ask next to On Manapool. Tick rows and press Push; listings Manapool already shows at that price and quantity are skipped. Prices reach Manapool only when you push or when the Schedule does.

Rules, Schedule and sealed pricing

  • Which rule wins: the highest-priority enabled rule whose criteria all match. A rule with no criteria matches everything, so a low-priority one works as a default. On Listings, a stack no rule claims uses the Default keep.
  • Profit vs cost (Listings Rules tab, Card pricing tab and Sealed pricing tab): a condition that picks stacks that would sell for more than they cost (is profitable) or for no more than they cost (is unprofitable), optionally by at least a $ amount per copy or a % of cost. It compares the stack's average cost with a price: on Bulk Price, the market price (so a rule's own new price never changes which stacks it picks); on Listings, the price it is listed at on Manapool, or its market price if it isn't listed. Stacks with no cost entered are skipped by any rule that uses it, even inside a NOT group.
  • Name overrides (Listings): an exception for one exact card name, like "always keep N of Shock". Checked before the rules.
  • Condition pricing (Listings): the percent of market price to ask for each condition when you export or push. A stack with a Bulk Price ask uses that instead. Changes apply on your next push.
  • Your Manapool listings (Listings): what Manapool has for sale now, checked against your listed quantity (all locations added up). Manapool is the source of truth: an edit you never pushed is corrected on the next sync.
  • Schedule (Listings, Card pricing tab): runs daily or weekly on the server, even with the app closed. With Auto-push on it sends new prices to Manapool with nobody checking. It doesn't change quantity, except that with Never list below my minimum profit on it takes listings under your minimum profit down, with nobody checking. With it off, a run only reports what would change. It touches only listings the chosen rule prices, and that rule must be switched on.
  • Never list below my minimum profit (Listings, Card pricing tab): with this Listings switch on, a listing whose Bulk Price ask is under your minimum profit over its average cost per copy is taken down instead of repriced, on Push and on a Schedule with Auto-push on. A scheduled run takes down at most 50; any others stay listed, unchanged, for the next run. Evaluate marks these Delist (below minimum profit), with the ask, cost and profit %. A listing with a stack that has no cost entered is never touched. Bulk Price never relists them; they come back only through Listings.
  • Waiting on price data and $0.00 (Listings, Card pricing tab): a stack with no base price yet is "waiting". Press Refresh prices on Inventory, then Evaluate again. Until then, and when a rule works out to $0.00 (Manapool rejects zero), it goes out at market price × the condition %, marked market × %.
  • Sealed pricing rules (Listings, Sealed pricing tab): match by an optional set code and/or name filter. No filters matches every listed sealed item. Highest priority wins.

7. The Buy List

In Simple mode this page is called Price watch and leaves out the moving-average columns.

This page is for finding cards to buy. You still buy on Manapool; the page points you at the listing. It has three tabs: Watch list, Restock (only once you've sold something or have wants) and Missing from a set.

  • Watch list: add a card from the results to track its price, owned or not. Compare "now" with a window of 7 days to a year. Near low means within 3% of the window's low. ↓ Below 60d avg means cheaper than its 60-day average. Click a card for its price history and a Retail price by vendor table (TCGplayer, Card Kingdom, Cardmarket, Cardsphere, Manapool). Press Target to set a price: "at or below" to buy, "at or above" to sell. When it's crossed, the row says Target hit, and the Dashboard and your daily digest mention it. A watched printing selling well above its usual pace on Manapool shows SELLING FAST (the same mark as on Movers, which counts every finish and language of that printing), with a note above the list, and the bell tells you on the day it starts (again only after a week without it). It needs two weeks of sales history before it can compare.
  • Restock: cards worth buying again. A card shows up when you've sold it through your Manapool orders, or a customer has it on their wantlist, you have none of it free to sell now (copies lent out, still on an open receipt or set aside for an order don't count), and it's selling well on Manapool this week: FAST (SELLING FAST, the same mark as on Movers), or the card is in the week's top 100 by copies sold. Sold 7d is copies sold on Manapool in the last 7 days by every seller, Rank the card's place in that week, You sold your own copies sold and Wanted how many customers want it. A row is one printing, or "any printing" for a wantlist entry that doesn't name one. Watch puts it on the watch list (for "any printing", its best-selling printing). The tab only appears once you've sold something or have wants, and shows the best 10 until you press Show all.
  • Browse & discover (the Missing from a set tab): pick a set or search a name. Tick Only cards I don't own for a missing-cards list. Each row is a card name. Low and High span every printing. Buy lists every priced printing and finish, lowest first, each linking to its Manapool listing. Friends shows whether a friend owns it (§8).

8. Friends & trading

Nothing is shared until you choose. Grimoire never moves cards between accounts. An accepted trade is a record that you agreed; you hand over the cards yourselves.

  • Friends (on Social): enter a username and press Send request. They Accept or Decline under Requests or from the bell. You can Cancel while it waits (up to 20 at once). Declines aren't announced. Then choose what each friend sees: nothing, a Summary (counts, total value, your fifteen most valuable cards) or Full (the whole card list). Until you choose, a friend sees what everyone else on the server sees. Sharing is read-only and never includes locations, cost or orders. Unfriend ends it for both of you.
  • Invite a friend (on Social): you get 3 invites unless an administrator has changed that. Press Create invite link, then send the link to a friend. It works once. When they sign up with it, you're friends straight away, with no request to accept, and the bell tells you both. Nothing is shared until one of you chooses, the same as any friend. An unused link stops working after 30 days and the invite comes back to you. Cancel gives it back sooner. An invite someone has used stays spent, even if you unfriend them later.
  • Share and trade from (organization accounts): pick whose cards friends see and your offers give from, My personal cards (default) or one of your stores (owners and managers).
  • A friend's collection: anyone sharing with you is under Shared with me on Social. Press View to browse, or start a trade from that row.
  • Offer trade: on the Buy List, the Friends column shows whether a friend with a Full share owns the card. Click it to see their printings, then Offer trade. The composer opens with a suggestion: a main card from you (a watch-list match if you have one, else the closest price) plus filler to close any gap in as few cards as possible. Edit or remove any line, add cards, or press Suggest filler to refill. New proposal on Trade Proposals or Social starts one from scratch. Send trade offer delivers it.
  • Trade Proposals: Incoming offers wait on you (Accept or Decline). Outgoing are yours; Withdraw one until it's answered. The bell tells you about new offers and answers.
  • Record a trade (on Card Inventory or Trade Proposals): for a trade made in person, with anyone. Under You gave, find your own cards and press Add. Under You got, search by name or Set + number. Both sides show their value at the Near Mint market price; a card with no price isn't counted. Save trade takes what you gave out of your cards and adds what you got, in one step. The cards you got take the value of what you gave as their cost, split by their value, so your profit figures stay honest. Undo at the top takes the whole trade back. Each trade is kept under Recorded trades on Trade Proposals with its date, who it was with and both sides' value. Delete record removes it from that list without changing your cards.

9. Order Fulfillment & Deck List

Order Fulfillment takes a Manapool order through pick, pack and ship, and a walk-in order through pick and hand-over. Deck List is a separate page.

Incoming orders

Orders appear on the Orders tab when you open or refresh the page. Press Generate pick list on an order, or tick several and press Generate pick lists. You land on the Pick tab.

Picking

  1. Open a ticket on the Pick list tab. To discard an open one, press the X on its row.
  2. Pull from ranks locations by pick priority. A line says short only when total stock is too low.
  3. Card not where Grimoire says? Press the button next to that pull to start a chase pick. Enter how many you found. Grimoire fixes that stack and moves the shortfall to the next best location.
  4. Tick Picked on each line as you pull it. The heading shows progress, like 7 of 9 lines picked. Ticks save on the ticket and show on your other devices; they don't change inventory.
  5. Press Complete pick list. This permanently takes the cards out of inventory (Undo reverses it right away). You're asked first if lines aren't ticked. It only happens once; a second device is told Already completed on another device - reload.
  6. If Pitney Bowes shipping is connected, a Weigh & Manifest step estimates weight. Edit or skip it. Then press Mark shipped.
  7. Marking a Manapool order's ticket shipped also marks the order shipped on Manapool. A table opens with a row per order: carrier (USPS to start; Set all carriers changes every row) and tracking number. Tracking is optional, except on rows marked Tracking required (Ground Advantage orders). Tick several tickets and press Mark shipped to do them together. If Manapool can't be reached or says no, the ticket stays on the Pick list tab as not sent to Manapool, with the reason and a Retry button; tick several of those and press Mark shipped to send them all again.
  8. If Manapool replaces, cancels or refunds an order after its ticket was made, the ticket says replaced on Manapool (or cancelled, or refunded) on the Pick list tab, with a red box on the ticket and in Warehouse Mode's pack step. It can't be completed, packed or marked shipped. An open ticket is discarded instead (it only set copies aside). In Warehouse Mode, cards already pulled for it finish as picked with the rest (the Finish screen says to set them aside), and its unpicked lines don't hold up the batch. A picked one, packed or not, has Cancel and put back: its cards go into a sorting tray (you choose which if you have several; one is made if you have none), each with its cost, ready to shelve with Warehouse Mode's put-away. The ticket goes, and its sale comes off Accounting, Statistics and the Dashboard. A sorting tray isn't a place you sell from, so nothing is listed while they wait there: put them away into a bin you sell from and the next Listings push can list them again. Any picked Manapool ticket has the button, for an order you know won't go out.

If a location has a sorting method (§2), its lines follow that order on screen and on paper. The button next to a pull shows where the card sits.

Picking several orders together

Tick two or more tickets and press Pick together. Each order stays its own ticket.

  1. Pull: one combined sheet, one line per card per location, naming each order it's for (Sol Ring ×3 — #1043 ×1, #1051 ×2). When it's pulled, press Complete pick lists.
  2. Sort into orders: a checklist for one order at a time, highest Mana Pool order number first.

Then weigh and ship each order from its own ticket. Dissolve batch takes the tickets out unchanged.

In a Store, a batch belongs to whoever started it. Only they can work it; others can open and print it. An Owner or Manager can hand it over with Reassign.

Walk-in orders (Order Entry)

Order Entry → Walk-in Order is for a customer at the counter. On the New order tab, search your stock, press Add on each card, and set the quantity. Each line starts at the card's market price; change it for a discount. A card with no market price needs a price typed in. Only stock in locations marked Sell from this location is offered, the same rule as Manapool orders, and it is picked from there too. Free counts only copies not already set aside for another order or lent out. A Manapool order holds its copies from the moment it's fetched, even before its pick list is made, whoever fetched it.

The customer is optional. Type the name of one of your customers, type a new name (they're added to Customers), or leave it blank for an anonymous walk-in. Then pick how the order is done:

  • Send to be picked: a Walk-in ticket goes to Order Fulfillment. Pick it at the desk or in Warehouse Mode like any ticket. Once it's picked, open it and press Hand over and record payment. There's no label, weigh or ship step.
  • Finish now: the cards are already in hand. Choose how they paid and press Finish sale. The cards come off stock at once.

Payment is recorded as cash, card or other, with the total. Grimoire doesn't run the card machine or a cash drawer, and doesn't add sales tax. The sale shows on Accounting as a Counter sale, with its cost of goods, and on Statistics' Sold vs market.

Until it's handed over, a walk-in order can be cancelled from its ticket. The cards set aside are freed; cards already picked go back to the bin they came from, with their cost.

Manapool: if the inventory's owner is connected to Manapool (your own account, or for a store, the organization's owner, so staff don't need a connection of their own), entering a walk-in order lowers each card's Manapool listing by the copies on the order straight away, so an online buyer can't buy the same copy. Only the quantity changes, never the price, and a card that isn't listed there is left alone. Cancelling the order raises those listings again by the same number. If Manapool can't be updated, the order still goes through and a warning names the cards to fix by hand on Manapool's site. A store whose owner hasn't connected Manapool gets a warning saying so.

The store's kiosk (Order Entry)

A kiosk is a screen in the store, such as a tablet on the counter, where customers enter their own orders. Order Entry → Kiosk is where a store's Owner or Manager sets it up; staff don't see it. The page has three tabs: Running kiosks, Staff PIN and Order limits, and it opens on the one you used last.

  1. On the Staff PIN tab, set the staff PIN (4 to 8 digits). There's one PIN for the whole account, shared by every store and kiosk. Give it to the staff who need to get a screen out of kiosk mode.
  2. On the device that stays in the store, sign in, pick the store, open Kiosk and press Start kiosk on this device at the top. Close any other Grimoire tabs on it first. Your own sign-in on that device ends when the kiosk starts.

Keep your password off the kiosk device

When you sign in on the kiosk device, don't let the browser save your password (choose Never or Not now when it offers). The sign-in page can come back on that screen, for example after a kiosk is ended, and a saved password would fill itself in. Turn on two-factor sign-in in Settings → Account too, so a password alone isn't enough.

Order limits, on their own tab of the Kiosk page, are for the whole account too: Most cards in one order (no limit unless you set one) and Most orders waiting per store (50 to start with). Leave a box blank for no limit. A kiosk with a card limit tells the customer how many they can add; once a store has that many kiosk orders waiting, the kiosk asks the next customer to see staff. Whatever the limits, a kiosk never sells more copies than are free to sell.

From then on the screen shows only the kiosk. Before typing anything, a customer can browse pictures of the cards in stock and narrow them by set, color, card type, rarity and price, with a Popular right now row of the store's cards that are selling fast on Manapool (just a label: no sales figures are shown). They can also search by name, set code or number, and the filters still apply. They only ever see the store's cards in locations marked Sell from this location that are free to sell, at market price. A card with no market price doesn't show, and neither does a copy sold in a Manapool order that's been fetched but not picked yet. Costs, customers, other orders and the rest of Grimoire can't be reached from it. The customer adds cards, enters their name and an email or phone number (both required), and presses Send order. The cards are set aside at once, the screen shows an order number, and it's ready for the next customer. An unfinished order is cleared after two minutes without a touch.

The order arrives on Order Fulfillment as a ticket numbered KIOSK-…, with the customer's email or phone on it, and everyone who works in the store gets a notification. It's also listed under Counter orders waiting on Walk-in Order. Pick it at the desk or in Warehouse Mode, then when the customer collects it press Hand over and record payment, exactly as for a walk-in. The sale shows on Accounting as a Counter sale. If the customer never comes, cancel the order from its ticket and the cards are free to sell again. Nothing expires on its own.

Manapool listings come down when the kiosk order is sent and go back up if it's cancelled, as for a walk-in. If Manapool couldn't be updated, the ticket says which cards to fix by hand.

Leaving kiosk mode: press Staff in the corner of the kiosk and enter the staff PIN. The screen goes to the sign-in page; nobody is signed back in. After several wrong PINs it waits 15 minutes, as sign-in does. An Owner or Manager can also end any running kiosk from Kiosk → Running kiosks. A kiosk stops by itself if whoever started it is no longer a manager of the store, and after 90 days, when a manager starts it again.

Deck List

Your decks, the cards in them, and a "what do I need to build this?" tool. The page has three tabs:

  • My decks: one row per deck, with its location, how many cards are in it (and how many its list calls for), its value and how many cards are missing. Click a deck to see its cards. Missing cards are marked short.
  • All deck cards: every card in every deck in one table, with the deck it's in. Search by card or deck name.
  • New deck: paste a decklist and build it (below).

A deck is any deck you built here, plus any location whose type is Deck Box. A built deck keeps its full list, so cards you didn't have show as missing. Building into a location that already holds a saved deck asks whether to add the new cards to its list or replace its list. Adding moves in whatever the combined list needs that isn't already there. Delete on a deck removes only the saved list. Its cards stay where they are, and a Deck Box location still shows as a deck. If a deck's location is deleted, the deck shows (location gone).

On New deck, pick what you are doing:

  • Build from my stock: lines match by name across every printing you own. Building a deck only moves cards you already have.
  • Add a deck I got: for a deck you just got (a precon, a purchase, a trade). Paste it, with a set and number where you have them, like 1 Sol Ring (C21) 263 (add *F* for foil or *E* for etched). The cards are added to your stock as new copies in the destination and the deck is saved under My decks. A line with no set and number (or one that isn't in the card database) gets a Choose printing… list and a finish list, with nothing chosen for you. Add the deck to my stock stays off until every line has a printing, and a note beside it says how many cards still need one. You can remove a line instead of choosing. A name that matches no card can only be removed. If the destination already holds a saved deck, you choose whether to add to its list or replace it. Undo takes it all back.

Buying missing cards: every missing card (in a deck's panel under My decks, and on the New deck preview) has a Manapool link and a tick box. Tick the ones you want and press Buy selected missing cards from Manapool. Grimoire copies them as a list (2 Lightning Bolt) and opens Manapool's paste-a-list page. Paste the list into its Mass Entry box and check out there. Grimoire can't check out for you. If your browser blocks the copy, the list is shown so you can copy it yourself.

Pick a Format (Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Premodern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper, Commander, Pauper Commander or Oathbreaker). Illegal cards get a warning. It also checks copy limits (4; 1 in Commander, Pauper Commander and Oathbreaker; basic lands and cards like Relentless Rats are exempt), restricted cards in Vintage, and deck size (60 minimum; exactly 100 in Commander and Pauper Commander; exactly 60 in Oathbreaker).

In Commander-style formats, press (the crown) on a line to make it your commander (two for Partners). Other cards are checked against its color identity, and it is checked for being allowed to lead. Pasted lists with a Commander section or marker set it for you.

13. Tracking value

  • Dashboard: total value, cost, gain, what's listed and allocated, and prompts for what to do next. Press Customize to drag tiles, hide one with X, switch Wide for a top 3, or Add a tile. Done keeps it; Reset restores the standard set. The Selling fast tile also says how many of your listed cards are SELLING FAST right now, each printing counted once. Click that line to open Inventory showing just those cards.
  • Movers: which stacks gained or lost the most value over a window you choose, plus a portfolio-value line on its own Portfolio tab. History starts the day your server first took a daily price snapshot. Click a row for price history and a Retail price by vendor table (Cardmarket is in euros and left out of the comparison).
    • Selling fast tab and Sold 7d column: Manapool copies sold in the 7 days up to Manapool's latest day of sales (it publishes once a day), every finish, condition and language of a printing together. ≥ means "at least". Pace vs usual and SELLING FAST compare separate sales, not copies, with the days before those 7, so one bulk purchase can't flag a card; they need two weeks of history. On a printing's row, Listed now (7d ago) shows how many copies are listed for sale now and, in brackets, the figure 7 days ago; a green down arrow means supply is draining, a red up arrow means it's growing. Click the heading to put the biggest drops first. Days left is roughly how long those listed copies would last: the copies listed now divided by the copies sold per day over the last 7 days, so ~2d means about 2 days, <1d less than a day and >1y more than a year. It counts every seller's listings on Manapool together, so it's a market-wide estimate, not a forecast of how fast your own copies will sell at your price. It's blank with fewer than 5 separate sales in 7 days, and the first click on its heading puts the shortest first.
    • MA30 and MA60: the middle price over 30 and 60 days, so one odd sale can't skew it. MA30−60 % is how far the first sits above (green) or below (red) the second. A dash means too few recent prices.
    • EDHRec rank: a lower number means more popular. The Signal filter finds cards whose rank is improving while the price hasn't moved yet.
  • Movers extras: the Portfolio tab's line (it has its own Vendor and Range picks; Vendor is the same setting as on the Movers tab) prices today's quantities at each past date, so it shows how value moved, not what you held. Today's top picks ignore your filters, show each card on one line at most, and skip trends of more than 1,000% (almost always a one-off repricing or bad price data). Explore lets you pick a category and sort any column.
  • Rises backed by selling: in the Signal menu on Movers, choose price up and selling faster than usual to keep only cards whose price rose and that carry the SELLING FAST mark in the Sold 7d column, so you can tell a rise with real buying behind it from one on a thin market. It looks at price rises only (it doesn't check for a golden cross). It needs about two weeks of Manapool sales history to compare paces; until then the table is empty and says it's too early.
  • Golden cross (Worth a look today): a card whose 30-day price line (MA30) has just moved above its 60-day line (MA60), a sign the price has started climbing. The list shows every card in the catalog that crossed in the last few days you choose (7 to start, up to 30) and is still above, with the day it crossed. At least N% above (5 to start) hides crosses where the 30-day line is only a hair above the 60-day one; 0 shows them all. Cards you own show their quantity; tick Show only mine to see just those. Cards under $1 are left out. The Golden cross line in Today's top picks is a steady climber: its price rose over several days rather than in one jump, and is still near its recent high.
  • Daily digest email: a morning summary of your previous day. Turn it off in your account.
  • Statistics: breakdowns by location, set, color, rarity, type, price band, finish and language, plus spend versus current value per receipt. Scope it with a query, or leave it blank for everything.
  • Collector view (Statistics): the share of each set's card names you own. A copy from that set counts; the same name from another set doesn't.
  • Sold vs market price (Statistics): what each printing sold for on real orders against today's market price. Only printings with a real sale appear. It compares by set, number and finish, not language.

14. Accounting

Pick From and To dates, or a shortcut (last 7 or 30 days, this month, last month, this quarter, year to date).

  • Tiles: Revenue, COGS (what the cards you sold cost you), Gross profit, Earnings (what Manapool paid out after fees), Receipt spend and Refunds.
  • Transactions: a revenue chart and one row per sale, receipt and return.
  • P&L statement: one row per month in your time zone, ending in Net profit.
  • Export CSV: downloads the tab you're on.

Where the numbers come from

Revenue comes from pick lists made from real Manapool orders, which record each sale price at shipping (§9), and from walk-in orders, counted as a Counter sale once handed over and paid (Walk-in orders). A counter sale has no fees. A hand-made pick or deck pull has no sale price, so only its cost counts. Revenue includes shipping the buyer paid, once the full order is fetched. Fees and Earnings stay blank for an order whose payment details were never fetched. Grimoire doesn't track sales tax.

Reading the CSV export

Date is UTC. Local Date is the same moment in your time zone, and is the day the P&L files the sale under. Use it to compare the two.

Reading the P&L statement

Gross profit is revenue minus the cost of cards sold, before refunds. Net profit takes off Manapool fees, Shipping (packing and postage, below) and refunds, then adds back Returned stock. It isn't the same as the Earnings tile, which doesn't subtract what the cards cost. Receipt spend isn't on it: stock becomes a cost only when it sells.

Shipping costs

Settings → Shipping holds what packing and posting an order costs you. It starts with typical costs, so change them to your own.

  • Supplies: each thing you pack with (sleeve, toploader, team bag, envelope, bubble mailer, box), its cost and its weight.
  • Packing rules: which package an order goes in and which supplies it uses: once per order, one per card, or one for every few cards (one toploader for every 4 cards). Each rule can stop at a number of cards, an order value, or both. An order uses the first rule it fits, top to bottom.
  • Postage: a letter pays one stamp, plus an extra-ounce stamp for each ounce (or part) over the first. A letter heavier than Heaviest letter moves to the next rule. Tracked parcels pay your own price for their weight. A Manapool order that needs tracking never goes as a letter.
  • Try it shows what an order would cost and which rule it would use.

Each Manapool sale keeps the shipping cost worked out when it's completed, so changing these later never changes past months. Sales from before shipping costs existed got today's costs once. Counter sales don't ship, so they have none.

Accounting opened for one card counts that card's share of each order's shipping, split by copies: what the buyer paid for shipping in its revenue, and what packing and postage cost you under Shipping cost, so shipping counts the same way it does in the P&L. Profit after shipping takes the cost off.

In a store, these are shared by everyone in it, and only a manager or owner can change them.

Simple mode hides Accounting and the Shipping tab.

Bin labels

On Locations, press Print bin labels to print a QR label for every location showing (use the search box or type filter to print fewer), or Print label in one location's edit window. The sheet is 3 across on letter paper, for the common 1 × 2⅝ inch labels. Print at 100%. Renaming a bin doesn't void its label.

Do I need labels?

Scanning

  • Phone camera: press Open camera and allow it. It stays on for the next bins. The camera only works on a secure (https) address.
  • Bluetooth scanner: pair it with the phone as a keyboard, ending each scan in Enter, and just scan. No button to press.

Pick and pack

Pick tickets and batches are made at the desk on Order Fulfillment. Warehouse Mode works them; it can't make them. The walk follows each location's Walk sequence, set in its edit window on Locations. Numbered locations come first, in number order, then any with no number by name.

  1. Press Pick and pack and choose a ticket or batch, then Start picking or Claim and start. From then on it's yours, and others see who has it. A batch nobody has started is open to any picker, unless a manager named a picker for it at the desk: then it shows only for that picker. An Owner or Manager can take over one that's started, in Warehouse Mode. At the desk, a started ticket is locked the same way.
  2. Can't finish it? Press Release. It goes back to the open queue, and what you've pulled stays on record, so leave those cards where the next picker can find them. A manager can release anyone's. The next picker is told how many are already pulled and by whom. Releases go in the Audit Log.
  3. Go to the bin it names and confirm it: press At this bin or scan the label. Any other bin's label is refused, and it says which one you scanned. If Require bin scan is on, you have to scan.
  4. Label missing or damaged? With the scan required, press Can't scan, pick the reason and type the name off the shelf. The reason goes in the Audit Log.
  5. Aisle blocked? Press Skip this stop. It moves to the end of the walk, and it still has to be picked or shorted before you can finish.
  6. Compare the card with the photo (tap it for a large one). Foil and other languages are marked in yellow. Press Picked.
  7. Tapped Picked by mistake? Press Picked so far, then Reverse on that line, and put it back. You can do this until you press Finish. Reversals go in the Audit Log. If Require bin scan is on and you've moved on to another bin, it asks whether the cards are going back: if they are, you check the bin when you get there.
  8. When everything is pulled, press Finish picking and check the totals it shows. This is when the cards come off stock, and exactly what you pulled is what comes off.
  9. Pack: anyone can pack once picking is finished, tickets and batches alike. Check one ticket against its list, or for a batch, tap each line into each order's bag, highest Mana Pool order number first. Press Packed, ready to ship. Order Fulfillment now says packed. Labels and Mark shipped stay at the desk. Only tickets picked in Warehouse Mode come here: one picked at the desk finishes at the desk. An order Manapool cancelled after it was picked shows Cancelled on Manapool and a Don't pack stop instead: press Set aside and take its cards to the desk, which cancels the ticket and puts them in a sorting tray.

When the cards aren't all there

Press Short: not all here and enter how many of that exact copy are really in the bin: the screen names the condition, finish and language, and other copies don't count. On-hand for that copy is corrected right away and logged. The rest moves to the next bin with free copies. That can be a bin you already passed: it sends you back to take the difference, and that bin needs confirming again. If no bin has any, the order is flagged short at the desk. What's already in your hand stays picked.

The bin also goes on the Bins to count list at the top of Cycle Count, with cards and sealed tracked apart, until it gets a full count of that kind. A count that matches the book closes it, and so does confirming the bin is empty.

If an order stops needing copies you've already pulled (say the stack's on-hand was changed while you were picking), it tells you how many to put back and where. With Require bin scan on, that takes the same bin check as a pick, unless you're still at that bin.

Cards in a tote, and the desk

While a ticket or batch has cards pulled in Warehouse Mode, even if it's been released, Order Fulfillment can't complete, discard, chase or edit it. It says how many are pulled and by whom. This keeps the stock book right: what's in the tote can only be finished or reversed in Warehouse Mode.

Counting a stack with cards in a tote: those cards have left the shelf, but they stay on the book until the picker finishes. So Cycle Count asks only about the shelf. Book qty is what should be there, and the row says how many are in a tote and who pulled them. Don't count those. The book becomes what you found plus what's in the tote, and Finish takes the tote off, so the book ends up matching the shelf. The picker isn't told to put anything back, and the order doesn't go short because of it. A count like this still takes the bin off Bins to count, including one that matches the book.

Chase on another ticket: a chase at Order Fulfillment on a different ticket works the same way, and its window says what's in a tote. Other tickets never send you for cards in a tote: they use what's on the shelf and then the next bin, the same as in Warehouse Mode. Book → Floor and Floor → Book check the shelf too.

Short because of a tote: if a ticket at the desk is short and copies of that card are in another ticket's tote, the line says so, for example short 2 (2 in a tote, pulled by Sam), on screen and on the printouts. The stock isn't missing, so there's no need to chase or recount the bin.

Cards pulled while the desk screen is open: right before a count, a chase or Complete, the desk checks the server for anything picked on another device, and the button says Checking… while it does. You can start typing as soon as a bin opens. If a card's stock changed while you were counting (a pick, a move or another device), that row clears, the message names that card, and Apply stops if you'd pressed it. Count that row again. If you were typing in it, the cursor has left the box, so click back in. Your other rows stay as you typed them. If the desk can't reach the server, nothing is applied and it says so. Check the connection and press the button again.

To cancel it: open it in Warehouse Mode, press Picked so far, then Reverse each line and put the cards back. On a released ticket you can do this without starting it, so anyone can, even if the picker has gone home. If someone still has it, a manager can release it or take it over first. Once nothing is pulled, discard it at the desk.

Find and move cards

Press Find and move, type part of a card's name and press Find it, then choose the printing. Each bin that has it is listed by condition, finish and language, with how many are on the shelf, and where the rest of the book is: for example 2 in a tote, pulled by Sam or 1 lent. So what's on the shelf, in a tote and lent adds up to the book. Look in a bin shows what's in one bin: scan its label, or type part of its name, which works with no labels at all.

  1. Press Move on the copies you're taking. Confirm the bin they're in, the same as a pick: a tap on At this bin, or a scan (or a typed name with a reason) when Require bin scan is on.
  2. Choose how many. The most is what's on the shelf: copies in a tote or lent out can't move.
  3. Choose the bin they're going to, by scanning its label or finding it by name, and confirm it the same way. A Bluetooth scanner's trigger moves them at once. A camera read only picks the bin: press Put them in to confirm, because the camera can catch a neighbouring label. The cards move the moment you confirm, so nothing is ever in transit, and it shows in Locations and Inventory and in the Audit Log, the same as a move at the desk. Just before it moves, the phone checks the latest stock (the screen says Checking…). If it can't reach the server it says so and nothing moves.
  4. A bin over its card limit says how much room is left and lets you Move anyway.

Copies set aside for a pick ticket that nobody has pulled yet can move. The picker's next look at that line sends them to the new bin. One move at a time: there's no moving a whole bin yet. A big bin shows its first 40 stacks; add a set code or collector number (for example island 1059) to narrow it, in a bin or in Find. Searches ignore apostrophes, hyphens and accents, and fire//ice finds Fire // Ice.

Cards only for now. A bin that holds sealed product says how many, and sealed is moved at the desk.

A Short correction and Finish picking check the latest stock first. Other people saving about other cards doesn't interrupt you and doesn't lose a count you've entered. But if the copies at your stop were moved or pulled elsewhere while you stood there, it tells you and shows the stop again, so a correction is never made on top of a move. Picked doesn't wait on that check.

Receive and put away

The desk opens a receipt first (Receiving on the floor). Press Receive and choose it. The first time, choose how you're working it on this phone; Change switches later:

  • Into a tray: everything you book goes into the sorting tray you choose (or Receiving, made for you). Put it away later with Put away.
  • Card to bin: each card you book goes straight on to its bin.
  1. Press Book a card, type its name and choose the printing. Set how many, the condition (it stays as you left it for the next card), and the finish and language when the printing has more than one. Press Book. Book sealed works the same way for sealed product.
  2. Booked a card by mistake? Press Undo on it, on the receipt's screen or straight after booking, and take it back out of the tray. You can undo your own bookings until they're put away or the receipt is closed. Undoes go in the Audit Log.

Booked cards are in stock at once but held: they can be put away and moved, but orders don't pick them and nothing lists them until the desk closes the receipt. Find and move says which receipt a stack is on.

Put away lists every sorting tray with something in it, including trays filled at the desk. Choose one (or scan its label) and it takes you stop by stop in walk order. Each card goes to the bin your Putaway rules suggest; sealed goes to the bin that already holds that product. Confirm the bin the same way as a pick: a tap, or a scan when Require bin scan is on. Scanning any other bin puts them there instead, no reason needed; a camera read asks you to confirm with a tap. Another bin finds one by name, and Skip for now moves a stop to the end.

Count a bin

Press Count. The list holds the bins waiting to be counted: bins a short pick said were wrong come first, then bins the desk sent to the floor with Send to the floor on Cycle Count. With nothing waiting, press Count next bin (or Count next sealed bin) and it gives you the most overdue one. Cards and sealed product are counted apart.

  1. Confirm the bin the same way as a pick: a tap on At this bin, or a scan (or a typed name with a reason) when Require bin scan is on.
  2. You get one screen per stack the book has there: the photo, set, condition, finish and language. It never shows how many the book expects. Type how many you see and press Next.
  3. If your number doesn't match the book, it asks you to count that stack again. If the second count matches the book, nothing changes. If it still differs, the second count is the one posted.
  4. After the last stack, press Found a card not on the list for anything in the bin the book doesn't have: search the name, pick the printing, set the condition, finish, language and how many. It's added to the bin and logged as found during a count.
  5. Press Done counting and confirm. The count is posted the same way as a full count at the desk (so the Audit Log shows each correction), the bin is marked counted, and it comes off the list. A count that matches the book, or an empty bin, closes the task too.

If a stack's shelf count changes while you're counting (the desk or another phone moves copies, or a new stack appears), nothing is posted and it asks you to count just those stacks again; the others keep what you entered. A picker finishing a ticket doesn't interrupt you.

If the signal drops

Warehouse Mode works live only. With no signal it stops with a full-screen message. Stay on that screen and walk back into Wi-Fi: it carries on by itself where you left off.
